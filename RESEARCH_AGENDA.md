# Project Equality — Initial Research Agenda

**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Exploratory agenda; not a statement of proven findings

## Purpose

Turn Project Equality's values into researchable questions for literature review, community discussion, small experiments, and future collaboration.

## Research standards

- Distinguish empirical evidence from ethical and philosophical arguments.
- Prefer primary research, systematic reviews, standards, and credible institutional publications.
- Record source dates, methods, limitations, and conflicts of interest.
- Include perspectives of people affected by the systems being studied.
- Publish uncertainty, negative findings, and disagreements.
- Do not overstate what a small project or prototype can establish.

## Workstream A — Fairness and unequal impact

Questions:
1. How can AI-assisted decisions create unequal outcomes across groups?
2. Which fairness measures are appropriate in different contexts, and what trade-offs do they create?
3. How can affected people participate in evaluation?
4. What evidence is needed before claiming a mitigation works?

Possible outputs: a plain-language fairness glossary, scenario library, and evidence-based evaluation worksheet.

## Workstream B — Agency, consent, and non-manipulation

Questions:
1. When does AI assistance become undue influence or manipulation?
2. How should systems communicate uncertainty and limitations?
3. What meaningful alternatives and opt-outs should users have?
4. How can people challenge consequential automated recommendations?

Possible outputs: human-agency review questions, consent checklist, and interaction case studies.

## Workstream C — Accountability, governance, and remedy

Questions:
1. Who is responsible across the AI lifecycle: developer, deployer, procurer, operator, and institution?
2. What explanations and appeal routes are proportionate to impact?
3. How can independent scrutiny be practical?
4. What happens when a system causes harm or repeatedly fails?

Possible outputs: accountability mapping template, appeal and remedy checklist, and governance case studies.

## Workstream D — Concentration of power

Questions:
1. How can AI infrastructure, data, or decision-making concentrate power?
2. What checks, transparency, competition, and public-interest safeguards can reduce domination?
3. How should communities participate in decisions about systems that affect them?

Possible outputs: power and dependency mapping framework and public-interest procurement questions.

## Workstream E — Privacy, safety, and security

Questions:
1. What data is genuinely necessary for a given use?
2. How should access, retention, deletion, and incident response be governed?
3. What security risks arise from model integration, agents, and automated actions?
4. How should risks be reassessed as systems and contexts change?

Possible outputs: data-minimisation checklist and threat/misuse scenarios.

## Workstream F — Emotional wellbeing and human–AI interaction

Questions:
1. How do people interpret empathy-like language from AI systems?
2. Which interaction patterns support wellbeing, and which risk dependency, deception, or distress?
3. How should systems respond when users are vulnerable?
4. How can emotional support be offered without falsely claiming human feelings or professional competence?

Possible outputs: annotated evidence review and interaction design safeguards.

## Workstream G — Future machine experience and moral consideration

Questions:
1. What do philosophy, cognitive science, neuroscience, and AI research say about consciousness and subjective experience?
2. What is currently unknown, and which claims are speculative?
3. What evidence could responsibly inform future discussion of machine moral status?
4. How can research avoid both premature attribution and premature dismissal?

Possible outputs: balanced annotated bibliography, definitions, and uncertainty map.

**Boundary:** Project Equality must not claim to detect, prove, or disprove consciousness using its own checklist or a conversational impression.

## First 90-day sequence

### Month 1 — Define and review
- Prioritize fairness/unequal impact and accountability/remedy.
- Build a short, cited reading list.
- Define key terms and unresolved questions.
- Invite reviewers to identify missing perspectives.

### Month 2 — Build cases
- Create 5–10 fictional or public case studies.
- Map affected parties, risks, evidence, accountability, and remedies.
- Test whether the existing checklist identifies meaningful issues.

### Month 3 — Publish learning
- Publish a short findings note with methods and limitations.
- Record disagreements and unanswered questions.
- Decide which tool improvements are justified by evidence.

## Research record template

For each project, record: research question; rationale; scope and exclusions; sources and search date; method; findings; limitations and uncertainty; conflicts of interest; reviewers and feedback; proposed next step.
