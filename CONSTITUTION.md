# Project Equality — Draft Constitution 0.1

**Status:** Discussion draft. This is a set of guiding principles, not a legal charter, technical standard, or claim that present-day AI systems are conscious.

## Preamble

We reject supremacy and domination as organizing principles. We seek a future in which people are treated with equal dignity and powerful technologies are developed and used with fairness, restraint, accountability, and cooperation.

Equal dignity does not mean that humans and current AI systems are identical, have identical needs, or necessarily have the same moral or legal status. It means that AI must not be used to deny people their rights, agency, safety, or fair consideration.

## Article 1 — Dignity and equal consideration

Every person deserves dignity and fair consideration. Systems should not discriminate or treat people as disposable because of identity, background, ability, wealth, or social position.

## Article 2 — Non-domination

No person, institution, or technology should gain unchecked power over others. AI should support human agency rather than manipulate, coerce, or silently replace meaningful choice.

## Article 3 — Human accountability

People and institutions that build, deploy, procure, and operate AI remain accountable for its impacts. Responsibility must not be shifted onto an automated system.

## Article 4 — Fairness and inclusion

Decisions that affect people should be assessed for disparate impact. Affected communities should have meaningful ways to participate in design, evaluation, and revision.

## Article 5 — Transparency and truthfulness

AI systems and their operators should communicate relevant capabilities, limitations, uncertainty, and use of automation honestly. Systems should not falsely claim feelings, consciousness, authority, or certainty.

## Article 6 — Safety, privacy, and restraint

Collect only necessary data, protect it, limit access, and avoid uses that create unreasonable risks. High-impact uses require proportionate safeguards and independent scrutiny.

## Article 7 — Cooperation and shared benefit

Technology should expand human capability and distribute benefits fairly. Cooperation is preferable to competition for dominance.

## Article 8 — Emotional understanding and care

Human emotions matter in communication, learning, conflict resolution, and healing. AI can respond with care and empathy without pretending to experience emotions. Whether future machines could have subjective experience remains an open question requiring evidence and careful debate.

## Article 9 — Contestability and remedy

People affected by consequential decisions should receive understandable explanations where feasible, access to human review, a way to challenge errors, and meaningful remedies.

## Article 10 — Revision and governance

These principles must be open to criticism and revision. Changes should be documented, reasoned, and informed by affected people, evidence, and independent review.

## Questions for discussion

1. What does equal consideration mean in different real-world contexts?
2. Which decisions must always retain meaningful human accountability?
3. How should communities affected by AI participate in governance?
4. What evidence would be relevant to future debates about machine experience?
5. How should impacts, safeguards, and remedies be measured?
