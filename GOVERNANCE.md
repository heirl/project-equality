# Project Equality — Early Governance and Participation

**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Interim working policy; not a legal charter or incorporated governance structure

## 1. Purpose

Project Equality should apply its principle of non-domination to its own structure. Decisions, research, technology, and leadership should be transparent, reviewable, and open to reasoned criticism.

## 2. Current stage

Project Equality is an early-stage initiative. It is not currently a formal membership organisation, charity, company, regulator, or standards body. No contributor should be described as an officer, representative, or authorised spokesperson unless that role is explicitly agreed and documented.

## 3. Interim decision principles

- Publish significant decisions and the reasons for them.
- Separate personal opinions from official project positions.
- Invite criticism before major changes.
- Record unresolved disagreements rather than hiding them.
- Avoid unilateral claims that a contested position represents everyone.
- Do not make financial, legal, or public commitments on behalf of the project without explicit authority.

## 4. Participation

Contributions may include ideas, research, issue reports, and proposed changes. Participation should be voluntary, respectful, accessible where reasonably possible, and open to different disciplines and perspectives. Contributors should not submit passwords, identity documents, private records, confidential employer information, or sensitive personal data.

## 5. Conflicts of interest

Disclose relevant financial, professional, institutional, or personal interests where they could reasonably affect an assessment. A conflict does not automatically invalidate a contribution; it should be visible and managed.

## 6. Research integrity

Research notes should distinguish observed facts, source-reported claims, interpretations, hypotheses, ethical positions, and open questions. Methods, sources, limitations, and material revisions should be documented. Do not claim independent validation unless it occurred.

## 7. Technology stewardship

- Prefer open documentation and inspectable methods.
- Minimise collection and retention of user data.
- Do not convert self-reported answers into verified facts.
- Keep humans and institutions accountable for consequential decisions.
- Publish known limitations and material incidents.
- Do not describe prototypes as certifications or guarantees.

## 8. Feedback and complaints

Feedback may challenge principles, research, tool behaviour, accessibility, or governance. Acknowledgement does not guarantee agreement or adoption. Where a concern identifies a credible safety, privacy, or integrity issue, pause the affected activity where practicable while it is assessed.

## 9. Changes to project principles

For material changes:
1. Publish the proposed change and rationale.
2. Invite comments for a stated period where feasible.
3. Summarise support, objections, and unresolved issues.
4. Record the decision, date, and responsible editor.
5. Preserve prior versions or a change history.

## 10. Funding and institutional relationships

Do not accept funding, sponsorship, or partnerships that compromise independence, create undisclosed conflicts, or require misleading claims. Before accepting money or entering formal agreements, establish appropriate legal, financial, and governance arrangements.

## 11. Future governance review

Before material growth, review whether the project needs a defined contributor and decision-making model, independent advisors, conflict-of-interest register, safeguarding and complaints process, financial controls, public reporting, and a suitable legal structure based on professional advice.

## 12. Accountability statement

Project Equality must not demand transparency from others while hiding its own limitations, concentrate authority without checks, or treat criticism as disloyalty.
