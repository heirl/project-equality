# Project Equality — Roadmap

Project Equality is being developed through three connected pillars: a public movement, an evidence-led research initiative, and an open technology project.

## Phase 1 — Foundation and identity (current)

- Publish the founding principle and draft constitution.
- Define the movement, research, and technology pillars in [Vision and Operating Model](VISION_AND_MODEL.md).
- Publish interim [governance and participation principles](GOVERNANCE.md).
- Publish the initial [research agenda](RESEARCH_AGENDA.md).
- Invite constructive criticism and identify unclear terms.
- Keep a public change history for important revisions.

## Phase 2 — Research and community validation (next 30–60 days)

- Prioritize fairness/unequal impact and accountability/remedy.
- Build a short, cited reading list.
- Invite a small and diverse group of reviewers.
- Create 5–10 realistic scenarios to test the principles.
- Record feedback, disagreements, and unanswered questions.
- Avoid collecting unnecessary personal or sensitive information.

## Phase 3 — Improve the technology (next 60–90 days)

- Improve the impact-review checklist based on scenario testing.
- Add stakeholder, accessibility, evidence, and uncertainty prompts.
- Distinguish self-reported answers from independently verified evidence.
- Make warnings understandable and actionable.
- Add test cases and document known limitations.
- Keep the website mobile-friendly and privacy-conscious.

## Phase 4 — Public participation and publication

- Publish plain-language explainers and research notes.
- Invite proposals and issue reports.
- Publish summaries of feedback and material disagreements.
- Consider moderated public discussions when there is capacity.
- Establish a transparent process for contributions and revisions.

## Phase 5 — Partnerships and sustainable growth

- Seek academic, civil-society, technical, and community collaborators.
- Establish conflict-of-interest and independent review practices.
- Consider funding only with transparent governance and appropriate controls.
- Consider formal legal structure only when there is a demonstrated need and suitable professional advice.

## Non-goals and safeguards

The project will not claim to certify AI systems, prove fairness, detect consciousness, or replace legal compliance, security reviews, audits, or democratic governance. A checklist can help people ask better questions; it cannot guarantee safe or ethical outcomes.

## Measures of progress

Track quality rather than vanity metrics:
- Principles clarified and revised with reasons.
- Research sources and methods documented.
- Number and diversity of substantive reviews, not just page views.
- Scenarios tested and gaps discovered.
- Tool defects and improvements recorded.
- Limitations and unresolved disagreements published.

## Working principle

Start small. Be transparent about uncertainty. Prefer testable improvements over unsupported promises. Apply Project Equality's principles to the project itself.
