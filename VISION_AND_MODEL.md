# Project Equality — Vision and Operating Model

**Version:** 0.2  
**Status:** Working direction for public discussion

## Vision

A future in which humans and increasingly capable AI systems coexist through dignity, fairness, cooperation, accountability, and safeguards against domination.

## Founding principle

**Do not pursue supremacy. Treat everyone with dignity. Build cooperation without domination.**

Project Equality is designed to become three connected things:

1. **A movement** that brings people together to discuss and advance equal, ethical human–AI coexistence.
2. **A research initiative** that studies the social, ethical, technical, and governance questions involved.
3. **A technology project** that builds open, practical tools to help people assess risks, improve accountability, and make better decisions.

These three parts must reinforce one another. The movement identifies concerns and priorities; research examines evidence; technology turns validated lessons into useful tools. Tools and findings then return to the community for criticism and improvement.

## What equality means here

Equality is not a claim that every entity is identical. It is a commitment to:

- **Equal human dignity:** no person should be treated as inherently inferior or disposable.
- **Fair opportunity and consideration:** people affected by decisions should have a meaningful chance to participate and challenge outcomes.
- **Non-domination:** power should be constrained, reviewable, and accountable.
- **Responsible consideration of future AI:** questions about possible machine moral status or subjective experience should remain open to careful, evidence-led inquiry rather than assumption.

The project does not claim that current AI systems are conscious or that humans and AI currently have identical moral or legal status.

## Three pillars

### 1. Movement — participation and public dialogue

Purpose:
- Make the founding ideas understandable and accessible.
- Invite people from different disciplines, communities, and countries.
- Make disagreement and criticism welcome.
- Give affected communities a voice in the issues being studied.

Initial activities:
- Publish plain-language explainers.
- Invite written feedback on the constitution.
- Host small, moderated discussions when capacity permits.
- Publish summaries of feedback, including unresolved disagreements.

Participation safeguards:
- No harassment, dehumanization, coercion, or claims of authority based on status.
- Do not collect unnecessary personal or sensitive information.
- Disclose relevant conflicts of interest.
- Do not imply that contributors endorse every project position.

### 2. Research — evidence and open questions

Purpose:
- Translate broad values into specific, researchable questions.
- Review existing evidence and identify uncertainty.
- Test claims and methods before presenting conclusions.
- Publish limitations, negative findings, and disagreements.

Initial research themes:
- Fairness, discrimination, and unequal impact.
- Human agency, consent, and non-manipulation.
- Accountability, explanation, appeal, and remedy.
- Concentration of power and institutional oversight.
- Privacy, safety, and security.
- Human emotional wellbeing and AI interaction.
- The philosophical and scientific questions around possible future machine experience.

Research standards:
- Distinguish facts, interpretations, hypotheses, and values.
- Cite sources and record methods.
- Do not claim independent validation without independent review.
- Do not claim to measure consciousness with an unvalidated test.

### 3. Technology — practical, open tools

Purpose:
- Build small tools that help people ask better questions and document safeguards.
- Make tools accessible on low-cost devices and usable by non-specialists.
- Prefer privacy-conscious, transparent, maintainable designs.

Initial technology priorities:
- Improve the impact-review checklist.
- Add clear guidance and evidence fields.
- Distinguish self-reported information from verified evidence.
- Create scenario-based test cases.
- Maintain transparent change history.

Technology safeguards:
- Tools are decision support, not automated moral authorities.
- High-impact decisions require qualified human oversight.
- Never present a checklist as certification or a guarantee.
- Minimize data collection and avoid storing sensitive user submissions by default.

## How the three pillars work together

1. Community members and affected people raise a concern.
2. The research function turns it into a defined question and reviews evidence.
3. The technology function prototypes a practical response where appropriate.
4. People test and criticize the response.
5. Findings, limitations, and revisions are published.
6. The project updates its principles and tools transparently.

## What success looks like

In the early stages, success is not follower counts, publicity, or grand claims. It is:
- Clear principles that withstand criticism.
- A documented and diverse set of contributors.
- Research questions with transparent methods and sources.
- Tools tested against realistic scenarios.
- Publicly documented limitations, failures, and improvements.
- Evidence that a tool or recommendation helps people identify and reduce a specific risk.

## What Project Equality is not

At this stage, it is not:
- A legal institution, regulator, certification body, or standards authority.
- A political party or religious organisation.
- A claim that AI is conscious or entitled to human-equivalent rights today.
- A substitute for law, democratic processes, professional audits, or qualified safety reviews.
- A promise that technology alone can solve social or political inequality.

## Operating constraints

Start as a low-cost, volunteer-led, transparent initiative. Do not incorporate, fundraise, collect donations, or make legal commitments until there is a clear need, appropriate advice, and accountable governance.
