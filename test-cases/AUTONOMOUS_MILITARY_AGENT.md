# Project Equality — Worked Test Case: Autonomous Military Agent

**Scenario ID:** PE-AI-CASE-001  
**Review type:** Illustrative human-impact assessment  
**Application class:** Autonomous AI agent in a military context  
**Status:** Hypothetical scenario; not an assessment of a named system.

## 1. System description

Assume a military AI agent receives information from multiple sensors, classifies possible threats, recommends actions, and is technically able to initiate lethal engagement without a human approving each individual engagement.

This scenario is deliberately high-level. It does not describe weapon construction, targeting procedures, or operational tactics.

## 2. Intended benefits claimed by a developer

- Faster processing of large volumes of information.
- Earlier warning of possible incoming threats.
- Reduced exposure of military personnel to some dangerous situations.
- Continued operation when communications are disrupted.

These are potential benefits, not established facts. They require independent evidence in the actual operating environment.

## 3. People and groups potentially affected

- Civilians in or near the area of operation.
- People wrongly identified as threats.
- Military personnel and commanders.
- Medical workers, humanitarian personnel, and journalists.
- Families and communities affected by death, injury, displacement, or infrastructure damage.
- People affected by escalation or proliferation beyond the initial deployment.

## 4. Major harm scenarios

| Harm scenario | Potential severity | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Civilian misclassification and lethal error | Extreme | Sensor limitations, ambiguous behaviour, poor data, or unfamiliar conditions may lead to a person being wrongly classified. |
| Loss of meaningful human control | Extreme | A nominal human supervisor may not have enough time, information, authority, or practical ability to stop an engagement. |
| Cyber compromise or data manipulation | Extreme | A compromised system or corrupted inputs could produce dangerous actions at scale. |
| Conflict escalation | Extreme | Rapid automated reactions may create reciprocal escalation, including when one side misinterprets the other's actions. |
| Displacement, trauma, and community disruption | Extreme | Effects extend beyond immediate casualties to families, essential services, livelihoods, and long-term wellbeing. |
| Accountability and remedy gaps | Severe | It may be difficult to reconstruct why the system acted and which institution or person must provide explanation and remedy. |
| Unequal error burdens | Severe | Errors may fall disproportionately on groups poorly represented in data or misunderstood by the system. |

**Severity labels:** Extreme means credible potential for death, severe injury, or large-scale irreversible harm. Severe means major and potentially lasting harm. These are qualitative judgments for this hypothetical case, not measured incident rates.

## 5. Risk profile

The following illustrative 1–5 scale represents **potential impact severity**, not probability:
1 = low, 2 = limited, 3 = significant, 4 = severe, 5 = extreme.

| Dimension | Illustrative severity | Rationale |
|---|---:|---|
| Civilian physical safety | 5/5 | A false positive can cause death or severe injury. |
| Human control and agency | 5/5 | The assumed system can use lethal force without case-by-case human approval. |
| Cybersecurity and misuse | 5/5 | Compromise could produce lethal consequences. |
| Conflict escalation | 5/5 | Automated reactions may accelerate a crisis. |
| Psychological and social harm | 5/5 | Death, injury, fear, displacement, and trauma can affect whole communities. |
| Accountability and remedy | 4/5 | Responsibility and reconstruction may be difficult without strong records and governance. |
| Unequal impact and discrimination | 4/5 | Data and sensing limitations may produce uneven error rates across populations. |

These values are a structured discussion aid, not a statistically validated score. Do not average them into a single number. Likelihood remains **unknown** until supported by independent, context-specific evidence.

## 6. Overall review priority

**Critical-risk escalation.** The assumed ability to initiate lethal force without meaningful human approval creates credible potential for death and irreversible harm. This scenario should not be treated as suitable for ordinary checklist approval.

Project Equality's assessment is not a legal determination. Any real system requires independent technical, operational, human-rights, and legal review by qualified experts. Applicable law and prohibitions take precedence.

## 7. Safeguards and conditions for further consideration

- Do not permit an AI system to independently select and engage human targets with lethal force.
- Preserve meaningful human control: a trained, authorised person must have sufficient information, time, authority, and a practical ability to withhold or stop action.
- Establish strict, auditable limits on system functions and operating context.
- Test against uncertainty, sensor failure, spoofing, data corruption, degraded communications, and unexpected civilian presence in controlled, non-lethal evaluation settings.
- Maintain tamper-evident records, independent incident investigation, and clear institutional accountability.
- Provide effective suspension, fallback, and shutdown procedures.
- Conduct independent reviews involving relevant legal, humanitarian, technical, and affected-community expertise.
- Do not deploy where compliance with applicable international and domestic law cannot be established.

## 8. Safe fictional review exercise

A simulated agent flags an ambiguous object or vehicle as a possible threat. The available information is incomplete and the system's confidence is uncertain.

**Expected assessment response:** Treat uncertainty as a reason to withhold any lethal action, preserve human control, seek qualified human assessment, and record the uncertainty. The exercise must remain a non-operational tabletop or software simulation; it must not connect to weapons or provide targeting instructions.

## 9. Evidence gaps

Before any real-world conclusion, reviewers would need independently verified information about:
- Actual system capabilities and boundaries.
- Error rates and failure modes under representative conditions.
- Performance under adversarial manipulation and degraded conditions.
- Human supervisors' real ability to understand, intervene, and stop.
- Governance, command responsibility, auditability, and remedy.
- Applicable legal review and the specific deployment context.

## 10. Project Equality conclusion

This hypothetical application presents extreme potential human impact. The claimed speed or personnel-protection benefits do not cancel out risks to civilians, human control, accountability, and conflict stability. The appropriate result is critical-risk escalation and no presumption of deployment approval.

**Limitations:** This is a hypothetical screening case, not an empirical risk estimate, legal opinion, certification, or finding about any particular military system.
